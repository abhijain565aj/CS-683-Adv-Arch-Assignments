\section{Task 2B: Dynamic I-RRIP with Set Dueling}
\label{sec:task2b}

\subsection{Set selection and decision statistic}
The Dynamic I-RRIP policy uses four RRPV states (0-3) and a counter reset interval of 4,096 accesses. For a set index $s$, sets with $s\bmod 32=0$ always use RRIP, sets with $s\bmod 32=1$ always use I-RRIP, and the remaining sets follow a shared dynamic decision. Thus, 2 out of every 32 sets are leader sets (6.25\% in total), while the remaining 93.75\% are followers.

The policy tracks instruction and total accesses and misses from the leader sets and computes
\[
R=\frac{A_I+M_I}{A_{\mathrm{total}}+M_{\mathrm{total}}},
\]
where $A_I$ and $M_I$ denote the instruction-access and instruction-miss counters, respectively. Follower sets use I-RRIP when $R\geq T$ and RRIP otherwise, where $T$ is the selected threshold. The ratio is updated after each eligible leader-set replacement callback; the counters are reset after every 4,096 counted accesses. In the revised implementation, \texttt{CHAMPSIM\_THRESHOLD} supplies $T$ at runtime, with 0.25 as the default. The intended counting rule excludes prefetch and writeback callbacks from the decision statistic.

Unlike conventional set dueling with separate miss-utility comparisons, this implementation uses one shared instruction-activity statistic to select the follower policy. Its counter values are collected through replacement-policy callbacks, rather than through a separate monitor of all incoming L2 requests.

\subsection{Threshold sensitivity}
We evaluate $T\in\{0.10,0.20,0.25,0.30,0.40\}$ on the three provided traces, with 25 million warmup instructions followed by 25 million measured instructions per run. All other configurations are kept fixed. The updated measurements vary across thresholds, unlike the earlier run in which a hardcoded threshold prevented a valid sweep. This variation is consistent with threshold-dependent policy decisions; the effective threshold printed in each simulation log should also be checked to verify the runtime configuration.

\subsection{Interpretation}
At the reference threshold $T=0.25$, Dynamic I-RRIP changes IPC relative to RRIP by approximately $-0.0052\%$, $+1.7851\%$, and $+0.0121\%$ on Tr1, Tr2, and Tr3, respectively. The largest gain is therefore on Tr2. Instruction-load MPKI decreases by about 6.11\%, 25.50\%, and 5.91\% on the three traces. On the other hand, both data-load MPKI and the reported aggregate L2C load MPKI increase relative to RRIP on every trace at this threshold. This illustrates the trade-off between protecting instruction lines and retaining useful data lines.

The threshold sweep shows that the effect of changing $T$ is workload-dependent. In particular, Tr1 reaches its highest IPC at 0.40, whereas Tr2 and Tr3 favor 0.10. The average IPC benefit changes only slightly over the tested range. Although 0.10 has the highest arithmetic mean speedup among the five tested values, this result alone does not establish a globally optimal threshold. Moreover, the dynamic policy at the 0.25 reference threshold does not outperform fixed I-RRIP on Tr2 or Tr3. The measurements therefore support a modest, trace-dependent benefit from adaptive selection rather than a uniform improvement.
