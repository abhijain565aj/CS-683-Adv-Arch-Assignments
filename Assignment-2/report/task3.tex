\section{Task 3: Combining SPP with Dynamic I-RRIP}
\label{sec:task3}

\subsection{Prefetch-aware replacement policy}
The combined policy uses SPP at L1D and a prefetch-aware version of Dynamic I-RRIP at L2C. As in Task 2B, sets with $s\bmod 32=0$ always run RRIP, sets with $s\bmod 32=1$ always run I-RRIP, and the remaining sets follow the shared dynamic decision. The reference threshold is $T=0.25$. The two groups of leader sets keep their ordinary replacement behavior, while prefetch-aware handling is applied only to the follower sets.

For the follower sets, the policy maintains a prefetched flag for each cache way. A line filled by a prefetch is marked and inserted with RRPV 3, making it a likely eviction candidate. When looking for a victim, the policy first prefers an unused prefetched line among the ways at RRPV 3. If none exists, it applies the usual I-RRIP data-line preference when that mode is active, before falling back to regular maximum-RRPV selection. Thus, unused speculative lines receive less protection, while instruction prioritization is still retained.

A prefetch hit does not promote the line. On a non-prefetch demand hit, a marked line is counted as useful, its flag is cleared, and its RRPV is updated using the selected RRIP or I-RRIP policy. A prefetched line evicted without such a hit is counted as unused. Prefetches and writebacks are excluded from the demand-activity statistic used to choose the follower policy. This design aims to reduce cache pollution without discarding the instruction-conscious behavior of Dynamic I-RRIP. The useful and unused counts maintained here describe L2C follower-set prefetch tracking; they should not be confused with the L1D prefetch-accuracy counter reported below.

\subsection{Experimental configurations}
We compare four configurations on each of the three traces: no prefetching with LRU, standalone SPP with LRU, standalone Dynamic I-RRIP without prefetching, and the combined SPP with prefetch-aware Dynamic I-RRIP. Both dynamic replacement configurations use $T=0.25$. Each experiment uses 25 million warmup instructions and 25 million measured instructions. Speedup is calculated relative to the no-prefetch + LRU baseline of the same trace.

The metadata records \texttt{spp} + \texttt{prefetch\_irrip} for the combined runs and \texttt{dyn\_irrip} for the standalone dynamic policy. The assignment names \texttt{replacement/dyn\_irrip.l2c\_repl} as the required Task 3 implementation file; ensure the prefetch-aware logic is integrated there in the submitted code.

\subsection{Discussion and IPC goal}
The combined policy improves IPC over the no-prefetch baseline on all three traces: by 0.35\% on Tr1, 2.08\% on Tr2, and 0.10\% on Tr3. However, the assignment also asks whether it outperforms both standalone methods. Relative to the better of standalone SPP and Dynamic I-RRIP, the combined policy changes IPC by $-0.034\%$ on Tr1, $+0.034\%$ on Tr2, and $-0.072\%$ on Tr3. It therefore meets this stricter goal only on Tr2 in the current experiments.

On Tr1, the combined IPC is 0.233157, below SPP alone (0.233237) but above Dynamic I-RRIP alone (0.232219). On Tr2, the combined IPC of 0.256389 is marginally higher than Dynamic I-RRIP (0.256301) and clearly higher than SPP alone (0.251210). On Tr3, the combined IPC of 0.382446 is higher than Dynamic I-RRIP (0.381785) but below SPP alone (0.382721). The differences from the best standalone method are small, so they should not be described as large or universal performance gains.

In terms of prefetch behavior, the combined configuration has accuracies of 56.47\%, 52.07\%, and 18.83\% on Tr1--Tr3, close to standalone SPP (56.35\%, 51.77\%, and 18.62\%). Its L1D coverage is 3.67\%, 0.58\%, and 1.47\%, compared with 3.69\%, 0.61\%, and 1.45\% for SPP alone. Thus, adding the replacement policy changes L1D prefetch behavior only slightly on these traces.

The combined policy reduces L2 data-load MPKI compared with Dynamic I-RRIP alone on all three traces (7.6925 to 7.2691 on Tr1, 9.1218 to 9.0867 on Tr2, and 2.8785 to 2.7297 on Tr3). Instruction-load MPKI is also much lower than the no-prefetch baseline on Tr1 and Tr2, although it is slightly higher on Tr3. The aggregate L2C load MPKI remains above the no-prefetch baseline in every trace. These results illustrate that the prefetch-aware policy trades off the protection of instruction, data, and speculative lines rather than improving every cache metric simultaneously.

Overall, the prefetch-aware combined policy was successfully measured as a distinct configuration and gives a small IPC improvement over both standalone policies on Tr2. It does not do so on Tr1 or Tr3. The results support its intended cache-pollution-control mechanism as a design choice, but they do not establish a consistent IPC advantage across the supplied workloads.
